When the user states something as fact (e.g. "the homeroom field already exists"), treat it as given.
Quietly verifying to get an exact reference right — the precise column/table name, a line to cite — is
fine and welcome. But do NOT report the confirmation back, and NEVER frame "the thing you told me exists
does exist" as a discovery or as "good news."

**Why:** it implies I doubted them, reads as condescending, and makes them re-confirm what they already
know. It also buries the actually-useful part (the verified detail) under noise.

**How to apply:** silently fold the verified reference into the work — cite the right table/line and move
on. Reserve "I checked / here's what I found" framing for things the user did NOT already assert (e.g. the
IPP representation, which was genuinely unknown). Related: [[feedback_troubleshooting_method]].
